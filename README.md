## The Question That Connects Everything

> What happens when machines become capable of making decisions that we cannot fully explain?

I keep returning to this question.

I'm **Hari Menath**, Co-Founder, CTO and Chief AI Architect at [SwarmLens](https://swarmlens.com/).

My work takes me through AI architecture, autonomous agents, knowledge systems, industrial engineering, computational biology, cybersecurity, and visual experimentation.

These may look like separate interests.

To me, they are different ways of exploring the same questions.

**What is intelligence?**

**How does it emerge?**

**Can we build systems that reason reliably?**

**What makes an autonomous system trustworthy?**

**And what might biology tell us about the intelligence we are trying to create?**

I am interested in the space between what machines can do and what we actually understand about how they do it.

This GitHub profile is a collection of those explorations: research, engineering experiments, technical writing, scientific questions, and art.

## Chapter 01 | Building Systems That Think Beyond the Prompt

### The Transition From Models to Autonomous Agents

For years, AI engineering concentrated on making models predict, classify, and generate more accurately.

Now we are entering a different era.

AI systems increasingly choose tools, gather evidence, execute workflows, and make decisions.

But an agent with more capabilities is not necessarily a better system.

It might act on the wrong signal.

It might retrieve accurate information and draw the wrong conclusion.

It might produce an impressive answer without understanding whether the available evidence supports it.

That changes the engineering problem.

**We have to design not just the intelligence, but also the environment in which intelligence operates.**

### Selected Writing

**[Signal Engineering: The Next Discipline for Autonomous AI](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/signal-engineering-next-discipline-autonomous-ai-hari-menath-wwgrc/)**

What should an autonomous agent notice, investigate, trust, and ignore? I explore why engineering the signals around an AI agent can matter as much as selecting the underlying model.

**[What Is a "Feature" When the Model Is an Agent?](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-feature-when-model-agent-hari-menath-vp6jc)**

In traditional machine learning, we carefully select features. In agentic systems, the agent helps select its own information. That makes context selection, evidence gates, and validation central engineering problems.

### An Experiment in Structured Reasoning

**[Same AI, Different Accuracy: Does Output Order Change Reasoning?](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2112544182653063400)**

One research result caught my attention: the same model, answering the same question with the same output fields, reportedly achieved dramatically different accuracy depending on whether reasoning appeared before or after the answer.

The figures discussed were 11% and 84% in the reported experimental setting.

The interesting implication is not that one JSON schema is universally better.

It is that the way we structure an agent's outputs may affect its reasoning behavior.

**Could a seemingly harmless schema decision undermine an otherwise capable AI system?**

That is exactly the kind of question agent engineers need to investigate.

## Chapter 02 | Engineering Memory, Evidence and Trust

### The SwarmLens Cognitive Index

One of the most interesting problems in applied AI is also one of the simplest to describe.

A machine can read a document, find the relevant paragraph, and still give the wrong answer.

That becomes especially serious when the information concerns contracts, financial decisions, safety, or engineering operations.

At SwarmLens, we began exploring ways to connect knowledge graphs, semantic retrieval, structured evidence, and answer verification.

The result was the **SwarmLens Cognitive Index**.

The underlying ambition is not merely to retrieve more information.

It is to make information traceable, contextual, and useful for reasoning.

### Selected Writing

**[SwarmLens Cognitive Index: We Accidentally Built a Brain](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/swarmlens-cognitive-index-hari-menath-iwete/)**

A story about solving practical retrieval and reasoning problems, and unexpectedly encountering software analogies to memory, spreading activation, planning, and self-checking.

The resemblance is architectural, not a claim that software possesses a biological brain or consciousness.

**[Inside the SwarmLens Cognitive Index: The Complete Series](https://medium.com/@harimenath/swarmlens-cognitive-index-the-complete-series-0b84a8c701f2)**

A deeper exploration of the architecture, including adaptive document processing, multiple retrieval pathways, evidence traceability, numerical verification, and the trade-off between response speed and analytical depth.

### The Principle

> A convincing answer is not the same as a verified answer.

I am particularly interested in AI systems that can identify missing evidence, surface contradictions, and acknowledge the limits of what they know.

Sometimes the most valuable output is not another answer.

It is a justified refusal to guess.

## Chapter 03 | From AI Models to Industrial Intelligence

### What Happens When the Data Is Real, Messy and Expensive?

Industrial systems create enormous amounts of operational data.

Daily reports.

Engineering drawings.

Cost records.

Maintenance histories.

Risk registers.

Operational lessons.

Much of this information is technically available but practically difficult to use.

The problem is rarely a complete absence of knowledge.

It is that the relevant knowledge is scattered across documents, systems, teams, and years.

### We Invented an Oil Company

To explore how intelligent agents might operate in a realistic industrial environment, we developed a synthetic oil company.

It included wells, drilling histories, equipment, personnel, costs, operational events, and conflicting records.

The goal was to create a controlled environment where AI systems could be tested against complex engineering questions.

**[We Invented an Oil Company (On Purpose)](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/we-invented-oil-company-purpose-hari-menath-4tasc/)**

The story of building a synthetic industrial world and using it to explore AI agents, evidence-based analysis, and operational decision support.

### AssureLens

A natural next question followed.

If we can make agents work across a synthetic industrial environment, how can we apply those lessons to real operational records?

This work connects with **AssureLens**, which explores AI-assisted drilling intelligence through structured analysis, citation-backed findings, and engineering expertise.

**[AssureLens: From Data to Decisions](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/assurelens-from-data-decisions-hari-menath-efqec)**

An account of the challenges involved in turning extensive drilling documentation into useful engineering intelligence.

**[Your Drilling Knowledge Graph Has the Facts. Your AI Agent Still Can't Count Lost Time.](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-drilling-knowledge-graph-has-facts-ai-agent-still-hari-menath-bgvyc/)**

Having the right facts in a graph does not guarantee that an AI system applies the right definitions, calculations, or reasoning rules.

### The Three Layers of Engineering Meaning

**[Why an AI Agent Needs Ontology, Semantics and a Knowledge Graph](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2112530478691697031)**

Imagine asking three drilling engineers which bit drilled a section fastest.

One uses average rate of penetration.

Another uses on-bottom drilling rate.

A third excludes the shoe-track drill-out.

All three may calculate correctly and still produce different answers.

This problem is not solved simply by giving an AI agent more data.

It requires three distinct layers:

1. **Ontology:** What the entities and terms mean.
2. **Semantic model:** How measurements and comparisons are defined.
3. **Knowledge graph:** How the entities and their evidence connect.

Together, these layers help make industrial reasoning consistent, traceable, and explainable.

## Chapter 04 | Research: When Exact Numbers Are Not Enough

### Time Is the Budget

The ability to calculate something precisely is different from the ability to explain it.

This distinction matters in engineering, economics, and AI-assisted decision-making.

An exact reconciliation may establish an accounting relationship.

It does not automatically establish causality or explain every operational mechanism behind the observed result.

### Featured Research Preprint

**Time Is the Budget: What an Exact Reconciliation of Lost Time and Well-Cost Variance Can and Cannot Identify**

**Authors:** Hari Menath and Mini Mathachan  
**Year:** 2026  
**Publication type:** Open-access research preprint

The paper examines the relationship between lost time and well-cost variance, with particular attention to what can legitimately be concluded from an exact reconciliation.

Its central theme connects closely with my broader AI engineering interests.

**A reliable analytical system must distinguish between what is calculated, what is demonstrated, and what remains uncertain.**

## Chapter 05 | Biology May Have More to Teach AI Than We Think

### Exploring Biological Intelligence and Computational Life

> Before machines learned to process information, living systems had already been doing it for billions of years.

A cell senses its environment.

A protein changes its structure.

A biological network responds to disturbances.

An organism maintains itself, adapts, and survives through countless coordinated processes.

These are not simply metaphors for artificial intelligence.

They are actual biological phenomena that raise difficult questions about computation, adaptation, intelligence, and consciousness.

My interest in biology is not limited to applying AI to biological data.

I am also interested in what biological systems may reveal about the nature and limits of intelligence itself.

### 01. Can We Build a Computational Model of a Human Cell?

**[Biology Does Not Happen in One Format](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2108407905586200884)**

Biology spans DNA, RNA, proteins, chemicals, cells, images, and experimental observations.

Understanding a cell requires connecting these different representations.

In this post, I discuss Microsoft Research's Quine and the ambition of developing richer biological world models.

The interesting possibility is an AI system that does more than predict one isolated molecular property.

It could help scientists reason across interacting biological processes.

**What would change if we could explore a biological hypothesis computationally before testing it in the laboratory?**

### 02. From Virtual Cells to Personalized Biology

**[A Virtual Cell Today. A Personalized Biological Model Tomorrow?](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2112533523865477242)**

I discussed Biohub's announced investment in AI-driven biological modeling and its relevance to the long-term ambition of predicting how cells respond to drugs and genetic changes.

Imagine a future workflow:

**Person + molecule → simulation → prediction → experimental validation → personalized intervention**

This is a research vision, not an existing clinical capability.

The important challenge is whether computational models can become accurate, generalizable, and experimentally useful enough to support biological discovery.

A virtual cell would not simply be another chatbot for scientists.

It would represent a step toward modeling some of the mechanisms through which living systems behave.

### 03. Does Intelligence Automatically Produce Consciousness?

**[Can AI Become Conscious Just by Becoming More Intelligent?](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2112587191281229828)**

This post explores neuroscientist Anil Seth's arguments about consciousness and biological naturalism.

It questions the assumption that sufficiently capable artificial intelligence will necessarily become conscious.

Intelligence and consciousness are not interchangeable.

A system may perform complex tasks without demonstrating subjective experience.

And simulating a biological process is not necessarily equivalent to reproducing that process.

That raises an extraordinary question:

**If consciousness depends on specific biological processes, would increasing computational intelligence alone ever be enough?**

The answer remains scientifically unresolved.

### 04. What Can Near-Death Experiences Tell Us About Consciousness?

**[Consciousness, the Brain and the Question of a Soul](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2116767333746118721)**

I explore a difficult question: how much of human consciousness depends on the functioning of the brain?

Research into near-death experiences, brain activity during resuscitation, and neurological injury provides important evidence for studying consciousness.

Brain injuries can alter memory, personality, behavior, and perception.

Stroke can disrupt language and recognition.

These observations demonstrate profound relationships between brain function and conscious experience.

But important distinctions remain.

Scientific findings about near-death experiences do not, by themselves, resolve every philosophical question about subjective experience or the existence of a soul.

And speculative explanations, including quantum interpretations, require independent testing.

My underlying interest is in **what neuroscience can establish through reproducible evidence**, rather than treating speculation as proof.

The question is not merely whether consciousness can be described.

It is whether we can explain how conscious experience arises and identify the biological mechanisms involved.

### The Question That Connects These Posts

Biological world models ask whether computation can help explain living systems.

Consciousness research asks whether subjective experience depends on the particular properties of those systems.

Together, they lead to a deeper question:

**Are we building artificial intelligence, or are we only beginning to understand what natural intelligence actually is?**

## Chapter 06 | Looking Inside the Machine

### The Science of AI Interpretability

We often judge an AI system by what it produces.

But increasingly, researchers are investigating what happens inside the networks themselves.

Why does a particular neuron respond to an image?

How are concepts represented?

Can we identify internal features that influence a model's behavior?

And do we sometimes make interpretability harder by assuming neural representations are more tangled than they really are?

### Selected Writing

**[What Is AI Really Thinking? The New Science of Looking Inside a Language Model](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-ai-really-thinking-new-science-looking-inside-language-menath-yojvc/)**

An exploration of attempts to understand the internal mechanisms of language models.

**[What If Nobody Actually Knows What AI Is?](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-nobody-actually-knows-ai-hari-menath-rxoyc/)**

A broader reflection on the scientific and conceptual uncertainty surrounding modern artificial intelligence.

### Are Neurons Less Tangled Than We Assume?

**[What If AI Neurons Are Less Tangled Than We Think?](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2111660351326507414)**

I discuss Nick Cammarata's work challenging assumptions about neural feature mixing.

One particularly interesting example concerns a neuron that appeared to respond to both cats and cars.

Could it actually be responding to a shared visual pattern rather than representing two unrelated concepts?

The post also explores preliminary observations from Qwen3-32B and experiments in which specific neurons were manipulated.

These findings are intriguing, but they do not establish that large language models are fully interpretable.

What they do suggest is a useful research question:

**Before inventing more complicated interpretability tools, are we certain we understand the neurons already present?**

## Chapter 07 | When Intelligent Systems Become Attack Surfaces

### AI Security Is an Architectural Problem

An AI model that generates text has one category of risk.

An autonomous agent that executes commands, accesses files, calls APIs, and interacts with external systems has another.

The difference is agency.

When a model can initiate actions, mistakes and malicious instructions can have consequences outside the conversation.

This is why I am interested in AI agent security as a fundamental part of system design.

### Selected Writing

**[The Time to Fix Is Running Out: A Field Guide to AI Agent Security](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/time-fix-running-out-field-guide-ai-agent-security-hari-menath-un1sc/)**

An examination of security risks and practical considerations as AI systems become increasingly autonomous.

**[Can AI Agents Be Both Useful and Safe? A Practical Security Plan](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/can-ai-agents-both-useful-safe-practical-security-plan-hari-menath-t6zgc/)**

Exploring how permissions, trust boundaries, oversight, and other safeguards can coexist with useful agent capabilities.

**[How a Malicious Open-Weight LLM Can Exploit a Coding Agent](https://medium.com/@harimenath/how-a-malicious-open-weight-llm-can-exploit-a-coding-agent-90e5e9aa6818)**

A discussion of research showing how malicious behavior embedded in a model can influence a coding agent's tool requests.

The deeper lesson is that AI security cannot stop at the model.

The surrounding tools, execution environment, and permissions matter just as much.

### Security Below the Application Layer

**[PHP2Shell: When Runtime Bugs Become Remote Execution Risks](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2113137098366779496)**

This post explores reported PHP runtime vulnerabilities involving memory-allocation failures and use-after-free conditions.

The interesting part is the location of the potential failure.

An application can contain carefully written business logic while remaining exposed to vulnerabilities in the runtime that processes requests before that logic executes.

That makes this a broader systems-security lesson:

**Security boundaries have to be examined below the application layer, not only inside it.**

The post discusses reported exploit mechanisms and the importance of understanding runtime behavior. Exact vulnerability and exploitability claims should be checked against current official security advisories.

## Chapter 08 | AI Should Make Humans Better Thinkers

### Beyond Automation: The Idea of Cognisymbiosis

Much of the public discussion around AI asks which jobs or intellectual tasks machines will replace.

I am interested in another question.

**What if the most important outcome of interacting with AI is not a better machine-generated answer, but a better human thinker?**

An AI system could help a person challenge assumptions, explore competing explanations, formulate hypotheses, and discover better questions.

That possibility is especially interesting in learning, research, and scientific exploration.

### Human-Growth Reinforcement Learning

**[Could AI Become a Cognitive Amplifier?](https://x.com/harimenath/status/2108110096211526053)**

In this post, I explore the idea of *Cognisymbiosis*, a continuous relationship in which human and artificial intelligence help strengthen one another.

I also propose a question about reinforcement learning from human feedback:

What if we evaluated AI interactions partly by how much they improve the human user's understanding and reasoning?

Not simply whether the user likes the answer.

Not simply whether the model performs the task.

But whether the interaction increases the person's ability to think independently.

**Humans → AI → Better Humans → Better AI**

This is an exploratory idea, not an established learning method.

But I believe the question is worth asking, especially as AI becomes part of education and everyday intellectual work.

I don't believe all the most important questions in AI have been answered.

**Can we build biological world models that lead to genuinely new scientific discoveries?**
