
## Chapter 07 | When Biology Meets Artificial Intelligence

### Exploring the Intelligence Hidden in Living Systems

> What if some of the most interesting ideas about artificial intelligence are waiting to be discovered in biology?

Long before machines learned to recognize patterns, living systems were already processing information, adapting to changing environments, and solving problems.

A cell responds to signals.

A protein folds into a functional structure.

A biological network coordinates countless interactions without a central controller.

And evolution produces remarkable complexity without an engineer designing every step.

These phenomena raise questions that I find fascinating.

**Is intelligence necessarily something that requires a brain?**

**Can we learn new approaches to AI architecture by studying biological systems?**

**And where do the similarities between artificial and biological intelligence begin to break down?**

My interest in computational biology connects naturally with my work in machine learning, autonomous systems, and AI research.

Biology offers something especially valuable to AI researchers: examples of complex adaptive systems that developed outside our engineering assumptions.

But the comparisons also have limits. A neural network is not a biological brain, and an AI agent is not a living organism.

Understanding those differences may be just as important as recognizing the similarities.
